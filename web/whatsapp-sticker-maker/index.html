Choose a clip that reads at sticker size

Start just before the reaction

Watch the source once and pick the moment that carries the reaction. A head turn followed by a smile, a cat noticing a toy, or a hand giving a thumbs up can usually fit in a couple of seconds. Leave a little space before the movement so the viewer can see what changes. End after the expression or gesture lands. Long pauses before the action make the sticker slower to read and leave less room in the file for the frames people actually want to see.

Set the edges on the timeline

The timeline spans the full duration of the source. The blue area marks the part that will be exported. Drag its left handle to change when the clip starts or the right handle to change when it ends. If the reaction is near the end of a long video, move the end forward first, then bring the start forward. The handles keep the clip within ten seconds. For a later range such as 21 to 24 seconds, enter both values in the time fields if that is quicker.

The fields and timeline update together for valid ranges. Use the fields when you know the exact times or when pixel movement on a small screen makes it hard to place a handle precisely. With a handle focused, the arrow keys move it by a tenth of a second. Hold Shift with an arrow key to move by one second. The GIF preview loops the selected time range after each adjustment. For MP4s, adjusting either edge shows the video frame near that time in the source preview.

Check how the last frame meets the first

After making the sticker, watch at least one full loop. A jump from an outstretched hand back to an empty frame can be the point of a reaction sticker. If the jump distracts from the joke, bring the starting and ending poses closer together or cut at a moment of similar movement. Watch the beginning on its own, too. A first frame with the subject in view gives the sticker a recognizable face or shape before the action starts.

Frame a GIF or video for a square sticker

Keep the subject inside the crop throughout the clip

The editor fits a square inside the source and centers it when the file opens. Drag the box sideways on a wide clip or up and down on a tall one. The crop position slider does the same thing if you prefer a fixed control. Use the shaded areas to see what will be cut off. A face may look centered at the start and drift out by the end, so check both edges of a moving shot and then watch the exported preview.

Give important details room to move

A close crop can make an expression easier to see, but it can also lose a hand, a pet's ears, or the punch line on a caption. For a clip with several people, select the person whose reaction matters and make sure the movement stays legible inside the square. If the subject crosses too far to fit, try a shorter stretch of the action. For text already baked into the source, check that each meaningful line remains inside the crop from the moment it appears until it is gone.

Start with a transparent GIF when the background matters

Existing transparent areas in a GIF can carry through to the WebP. A regular video keeps the scene behind the subject, including walls, shadows, or a green screen. Preview the shape against both light and dark backgrounds when the sticker has a thin outline or dark edges. If your next step requires an edited GIF instead of a sticker file, use the GIF Cropper to keep the output in GIF format, or use the Circle GIF Cropper for a rounded GIF with transparent corners.

Fit the animation within 500 KB

Keep the portion with the clearest motion

WhatsApp's animated sticker format uses WebP with a 512 × 512 canvas, a 500 KB file limit, and a ten second duration limit. File size depends on what changes from frame to frame. A simple illustration moving across a flat background often leaves more room than a camera pan through a crowd. This sticker maker reduces quality or drops frames as needed to fit within the size limit. Look for a notice next to the result if frames were dropped, then watch the fastest movement to see how it looks. WhatsApp’s animated sticker specifications

Try a short version before a long one

If a six second clip spends four seconds setting up the reaction, make a two or three second version around the payoff. You may get a smaller file and keep more detail in the moment that matters. A person turning quickly, a spinning object, or a flash of text can look jumpy when the export drops frames. In those cases, trimming unused time usually gives you a better comparison than repeatedly processing the full length. Compare the output size and motion after each change.

Check the file format if the source will not open

The editor accepts animated GIFs and H.264 video inside MP4 files, up to 20 MB. An MP4 extension by itself does not identify the video codec. If the editor asks for H.264, export the source with that codec in your video editor, then choose the new file. MP4 sources must be no longer than 30 seconds before you load them. If a long recording contains the reaction you want, save a short excerpt first and use the timeline here to make the final cut. Large video dimensions can also hit browser processing limits, so try a 1080p copy if the original is rejected.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert a GIF to a WhatsApp sticker?

Yes. Choose an animated GIF and export it as animated WebP. To keep working in GIF format, use the GIF Cropper or GIF Compressor.

Can I convert a GIF or video to a sticker without an app on a PC?

Yes. For GIF to sticker conversion, choose an animated GIF. For video to sticker conversion, choose an H.264 MP4 in a browser that supports H.264 decoding with WebCodecs. Trim and crop the clip, then download the animated WebP directly from the browser. To use it in WhatsApp, see the sticker pack instructions.

How long can my video be?

Choose an H.264 MP4 up to 30 seconds and 20 MB. The first 3 seconds are selected by default, and you can trim any clip up to 10 seconds. MP4 conversion requires a browser with H.264 support in WebCodecs.

Will my sticker have a transparent background?

The editor preserves transparency already present in a GIF. A normal MP4 keeps its original background inside the square crop.

Are my files uploaded?

Processing happens in your browser. The GIF or video stays on your device.

Does a video sticker include sound?

The WebP stores image frames. Choose a moment that makes sense from the movement, expression, or text already visible in the source.

Can I use both the timeline and the time fields?

Yes. Drag a handle to make a rough cut, then edit its time in seconds for a precise adjustment. Select Reset trim and crop to return to the default clip and centered square.
